I started a World War 2 faceless YouTube channel. Finding the niche was the easy part.

Then I got stuck. And I dropped the channel.

I don't have numbers on how many faceless channels quit early. Nobody outside YouTube does. But if you've been around faceless YouTube for a while, you've seen it. Channels post for a few months, then go quiet. I was one of them.

Here's what happened to me, and what I think happens to a lot of people.

## The niche was never the hard part

Every guide tells you the niche is the big decision. Pick the right one and the rest follows.

It didn't work like that for me. [NEEDS MAC INPUT] How you picked World War 2, and how long it took.

I had a niche. I had a channel. I had a few videos out. [NEEDS MAC INPUT] Roughly how many videos you published and over how long.

Then came the question nobody had prepared me for: _what now?_

## Reason 1: No idea what's working for anyone else

I could see my competitors. I could see their subscriber counts. What I couldn't see was which of their videos were actually working.

Some videos on a channel pull in new viewers. Most don't. Without knowing which is which, you copy the wrong things. You make another version of a video that only did well because the channel was already big.

That's the gap outliers fill. [More on that here](/blog/what-is-an-outlier-video). Back then I didn't have a way to find them.

## Reason 2: No way to see what's breaking out

Topics move. Something in your niche starts getting views this month that wasn't getting views last month.

If you find it early, you can make a video while it's still growing. If you find it late, you're the fifteenth channel covering it.

I had no system for this. I found topics by scrolling and guessing. [NEEDS MAC INPUT] A specific topic you wish you'd caught earlier, if there is one.

## Reason 3: Every video started from zero

This one wore me down the most.

Each new video meant a blank page. New topic research. New script structure. New title ideas. New thumbnail idea. Nothing carried over from the last video.

When something worked, I didn't have a way to turn it into a repeatable pattern. So I couldn't build on wins. I just started over.

That's exhausting. And exhausting is how channels die. Not in one bad week. In a slow pile-up of weeks where every video costs as much as the first one.

## Reason 4: The early silence

New channels get very few views at the start. That's normal. But it's brutal when you can't tell the difference between "this is normal" and "I'm doing it wrong."

With no signal, every quiet video feels like proof you should quit. [NEEDS MAC INPUT] What the quiet stretch felt like, in one or two honest lines.

## What I'd do differently now

If I started the channel again today:

1. **Track 5 to 10 competitors from day one.** Not their subscriber counts. Their outliers.
2. **Keep a list of breakout topics.** Check it every week before picking the next video.
3. **Turn every win into a template.** When a video works, for me or a competitor, write down the title structure, the hook, the script shape. Reuse it.
4. **Plan a month ahead.** A calendar with topics already picked means no blank-page mornings.
5. **Judge by pattern, not by one video.** Decide what's working after 10 videos, not after 2.

None of this is clever. It's a system. That's the part I was missing.

I dropped the channel. Then I decided this tool should exist.
