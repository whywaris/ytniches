Most new faceless channels don't fail because the videos are bad. They fail because there's no plan, so every day starts with "what should I make?"

This plan fixes that for your first 30 days. By the end you'll have published videos, a list of what works in your niche, and a calendar for month two.

It assumes you've already picked a niche. If you haven't, [start with this post](/blog/find-a-faceless-niche-that-isnt-saturated).

## The rule for the whole month

Pick a publishing pace you can keep, and keep it.

One video a week is a fine start. Two is better if you can sustain it. Don't promise yourself daily uploads. Missing a promise you made to yourself is how the quitting starts.

This plan uses **two videos a week**. Halve it if you need to. The structure still works.

## Week 1: Research (days 1–7)

No publishing this week. You're building the base everything else runs on.

**Days 1–2: Pick your competitors.** Find 5 to 10 channels in your niche. Mix sizes. Include a few small channels that are clearly growing. Those are the most useful to learn from.

**Days 3–4: Find their outliers.** Go through each channel's recent videos. Mark every video that got at least three times the channel's usual views. [Here's how to spot them](/blog/what-is-an-outlier-video).

**Day 5: Find the patterns.** Put all the outliers side by side. What topics repeat? What title structures? How long are they? Write down what you see.

**Days 6–7: Build your idea list.** Write 30 video ideas based on those patterns. Your angle, their proven topics. Rank them. Your top 6 become this month's videos. Keep the next few as spares.

## Week 2: Build your template and publish (days 8–14)

**Days 8–9: Make a script template.** Look at how the outliers open. Most good faceless videos follow a shape: a hook, a setup, the main content, a payoff. Write that shape down as a fill-in template.

**Day 10: Make a thumbnail template.** Same idea. One layout you can reuse with a new image and a few words. Consistency helps viewers recognize you.

**Days 11–12: Make video 1.** Use your templates. Time how long each part takes. You'll need those numbers later.

**Days 13–14: Publish video 1, make video 2.** Don't wait for video 1 to "do well" before starting video 2. Early views mean very little.

## Week 3: Publish on schedule (days 15–21)

Now it's just the routine.

- **Monday:** Script video 3.
- **Tuesday:** Visuals and voiceover for video 3. Publish.
- **Wednesday:** Title and thumbnail tests. Write 5 title options for your next video before choosing one.
- **Thursday:** Script video 4.
- **Friday:** Visuals and voiceover for video 4. Publish.
- **Weekend:** Check your competitors for new outliers. Add anything useful to your idea list.

The weekend check matters. Your niche moves. A topic breaking out this week is worth jumping on while it's still growing.

## Week 4: Publish and review (days 22–30)

Keep the same routine for videos 5 and 6.

Then on days 29 and 30, stop and review. Not your view counts. They're too small to mean much yet. Review your process:

- How long did each video actually take? Can you keep this pace for three more months?
- Which part took the longest? Can a template cut it down?
- Which of your videos did best relative to the others, even if all the numbers are small?
- Did any competitor post a new outlier you should respond to?

Then use your idea list to fill a calendar for month two. Every slot gets a topic before the month starts.

## What you'll have at day 30

- Around 6 published videos.
- A tracked list of competitors and their outliers.
- A script template and a thumbnail template.
- An idea list that keeps growing.
- A calendar for next month that's already filled.

That's not a viral channel. It's something better at this stage: a system you can keep running.
